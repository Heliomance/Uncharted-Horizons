# Rows available two or more eras early: a review (15 October 2026)

Tamsin asked which breakthroughs can be had an era or more before their own era, and then for this review: for each row whose prerequisites are two or more eras older than the row, what it represents, what actually held it back in history, and whether its prerequisites capture that. The worked example was Petroleum Discovery (MI07): petroleum was known and used from antiquity, and what made it an industry in the 1850s was steam-powered drilling and the kerosene lamp, neither of which its prerequisites (Deep Shaft Mining, Geological Observation, both Ancient) contain.

**Method.** `design/simulator/power/early_availability.py` lists the 38 rows. Five researchers took a share each, by theme, and checked history against sources where unsure (links in the per-row notes). Every proposed prerequisite was then checked against the document's rules: no prerequisite from a later era than the row; a CORE row gains only CORE prerequisites; the prerequisite exists in every world the row does; no cycles. The battery passes unchanged with all of them applied. History is the researchers', spot-checked, not verified line by line.

## 5. The three held back (follow-up, 15 October)

Tamsin adopted the other sixteen in v3.115 and asked about these three. Outcome (v3.116): the University change in `UNIV=2`'s form, Scientific Method after the University and the press; Comparative Anatomy merged into Anatomy Study; the Windmill's Water Mill withdrawn.

### Scientific Method after the University, with the University no longer after the Printing Press

- **History.** The University's requirement of the press is backwards. Bologna (1088), Paris (c. 1150–1200) and Oxford (from 1167) came three centuries before Gutenberg (c. 1450), and the universities' own book trade, the stationers' *pecia* copying system of the 13th century, was part of the market the press later served. Older institutions of higher learning are earlier still: the Pandidakterion of Constantinople (425), Nalanda (5th c.), al-Qarawiyyin (859) and al-Azhar (970), all Early Medieval, which §2 places in the Classical era.
- **Structure** (`UNIV=1` in `scratch_patch.py`). The University needs only Writing and Formal Logic, so it is available from the Classical era, one era early. Scientific Method moves from the Classical era (two early) to the Medieval (one early). No other row's availability moves. Rows that cannot be had without the University: 3 → 310 (every row after Scientific Method). Without the Printing Press: 5 → 1 (Public Education, which names it directly). The battery passes unchanged, and the Experimental Laboratory's University is now certain to come first.
- **What it gives up.** The review named two institutional enablers of the method: the universities, and the press, which made observations fixed, cumulative and checkable. Under this variant the method needs only the first. `UNIV=2` keeps both (Scientific Method after the University and the press): the press then becomes an ancestor of 309 rows again, with no change to any row's availability, since both are Medieval.
- **Measured** (`UNIV=1` against the v3.115 document, both seed sets, 5,400 games): victory medians within one turn for every player in every world, and 19 unfinished games against 31, all STANDARD build-list ideas that never occurred. The University variant costs the FTL race nothing; the drop in unfinished games is probably noise. Results: `…_cmpU1…` and `…_cmpU1s2…`.

### Comparative Anatomy (O07)

- **What it is.** Named for the discipline of comparing animal bodies, which as a discipline is Cuvier's (*Leçons d'anatomie comparée*, 1800–05). As a Classical row it stands for its beginnings: Alcmaeon's and Aristotle's animal dissections, and Galen's anatomy, which was largely of apes and pigs.
- **What it does.** Nothing of its own. Its only benefit is opening the Medicine focus, which Herbal Medicine plus Writing also open, and normally first: Writing is an Ancient CORE row, while Comparative Anatomy is a Classical row whose price carries the era premium until the Classical era (reasoned, not measured: the simulator ignores focus gates). Structurally it is a single gate: its only dependent is Anatomy Study (MD03), and through it 36 rows (Germ Theory, Vaccination, the Medical School).
- **How it got here.** The first draft (13 February) made it STANDARD, with the Medicine focus, better unit healing and an Anatomist specialist. It is CORE because it opens a focus, the first of v3.59's criteria. On 27 September the consolidation cut its building, the Anatomy Theatre (a Scientist-slot shell), and kept the row because it "unlocks Medicine".
- **Verdict:** not justified as it stands. It duplicates Anatomy Study, which is Classical too and covers the same tradition (Herophilus and Erasistratus; Galen).
- **Suggested fix:** merge it into Anatomy Study: Anatomy Study requires Animal Behavior Study (O03) + Herbal Medicine (G03) + Natural Philosophy (O06, which brings Writing), and the Medicine focus opens with Herbal Medicine + Writing alone. Anatomy Study stays available from the Classical era, as today, and nothing downstream moves. The tree loses one CORE row (~544). If a separate row is wanted instead, it would need a benefit of its own (v3.84), and its name fits the Early Modern discipline better than a Classical one.

### The Windmill (MC04)

- **History.** Partly. The European post mill (Weedley, Yorkshire, 1185) took the water mill's right-angle gearing and millstones and replaced the wheel with sails, so in Europe the geared water mill was its technical parent. But the windmill spread where water power was lacking or taken (flat, dry or freezing country, and streams whose mills a lord owned), and the earlier Persian mill of Sistan (first secure records in the 10th century) needed no gearing at all: its sails turned a vertical shaft straight into the millstone. What both needed was the mill and the sail, and the row's present prerequisites, Gearing and Sail Invention, already say exactly that.
- **Verdict:** the Water Mill proposal is withdrawn; the remaining earliness is demand, for the time limit (which would open it in the Classical era, where the Sistan mills fall).
- **One change worth making:** today the Windmill already needs water, indirectly. Sail Invention needs the Dugout Canoe, which needs the Reed/Log Raft and its "Coastal or River location". Civ V's Windmill is the mill for cities with no river, so the dry inland civilisation that most needs it cannot have it. Weaving (TX01, CORE, Primitive) in place of Sail Invention keeps the cloth (Sail Invention itself needs Weaving), drops the coast, and changes no availability: the row stays Ancient-available. The wider problem, terrain conditions on CORE rows that gate the industrial chain and the FTL race, is in `design/notes/deferred_tasks.md`, *Decide the terrain conditions on CORE rows*.

## Side findings

- **The simulator ignores focus gates:** it treats a focus as open once any of its rows is discoverable.
- **Two Ancient labels look early:** Paper Making (TX10) and Gearing (MC03) are historically Classical (paper is Han; geared machines Hellenistic, as Gearing's own text says). Much of the Printing Press's earliness comes from them.
- **Calculus's "Unlocks Mechanics focus (advanced)" opens nothing:** Mechanics already opens with Geometry and Quarrying.
- **The Cotton Gin (TX05) bundles two centuries:** its prerequisites describe the medieval geared roller gin, its building the 18th-century spinning mill.
- **Coal Discovery (MI06) bundles an Ancient discovery with a Medieval industry,** and Steel Production lists it as an option, so it cannot take Steel as a prerequisite.

## Per-row notes

The researchers' accounts, lightly edited. Each gives what the row represents, the history, and the reasoning; sources are linked where a date was checked.

### Comparative Anatomy (O07) — CORE, Classical, now available from Primitive

- **Represents:** The systematic comparison of animal bodies by dissection. Its only benefit is opening the Medicine focus; the other route is Herbal Medicine (G03) plus Writing (SO04). It is also required by Anatomy Study (MD03), which grants the Medical School.
- **History:** Precursors: butchery and hunting lore (Palaeolithic onwards); Mesopotamian liver divination with clay models of sheep livers (c. 1900–1600 BC); Egyptian embalming and the Edwin Smith papyrus (c. 1600 BC). The row's own achievement: Alcmaeon of Croton's animal dissections (c. 500 BC) and Aristotle's History of Animals and Parts of Animals (c. 350–322 BC), the founding works of comparative anatomy. Herophilus and Erasistratus (Alexandria, 3rd c. BC) and Galen (2nd c. AD) continued the work. Once animal lore and herbal medicine existed, what was missing was not a tool but a habit of mind: written, systematic inquiry into nature.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite (needs Tamsin): add Natural Philosophy (O06, CORE, Classical), then available from Classical (confidence high).
- **Why:** Comparative anatomy began within Greek natural philosophy, in Alcmaeon's and Aristotle's dissections. Natural Philosophy (O06) already stands for that tradition, so it is the missing prerequisite. It is CORE and Classical (the row's own era), exists in every world, and requires only Star Tracking (O01) and Writing (SO04), so there is no cycle.
- **Notes:** This change has a knock-on effect. Natural Philosophy requires Writing, and Comparative Anatomy already requires Herbal Medicine, so Herbal Medicine plus Writing will always have opened Medicine before Comparative Anatomy arrives. Its one benefit would then never take effect. Under the v3.84 rule (every breakthrough carries a benefit) it needs a benefit of its own, or it could be merged into Anatomy Study (MD03). A civilisation without Writing, such as a Quipu civilisation (SO09), also loses its early route into Medicine. Dependents: Anatomy Study (MD03) then arrives in its own era. Traditional Medicine Integration (MD10, UNIQUE, Classical) also waits for Natural Philosophy, which is likewise its own era. The other dependents of Anatomy Study (MD04, MD05, MD08, MD15, CP11, GN04, GN12) belong to later eras and are unaffected. Surgical Techniques (MD01) is unaffected.

### Falconry (AH07) — UNCOMMON, Classical, now available from Primitive

- **Represents:** Hunting with trained raptors, recast as a military bonus: +1 Sight for Ranged units, and new Ranged units start with 15 XP.
- **History:** Precursors: knowledge of raptors and their prey (prehistoric). The row's own achievement is training raptors to hunt, and its origin is uncertain. A raptor on a 3rd-millennium-BC sherd from Tell Chuera (Syria) and Assyrian reliefs (8th c. BC) have been claimed as evidence, but both claims are contested. The first firm records are Chinese, from the Chu kingdom before 700 BC. Greek writers report the practice in India and Thrace (4th c. BC). It spread across Eurasia in the 4th–5th c. AD (Japan; Europe with the Huns and Alans) and peaked as a noble sport in medieval Europe and the Islamic world (Frederick II's treatise, 1240s). Once animal lore existed, it needed no new technique beyond leather jesses. What it did need was an elite able to keep full-time falconers, since a trained hawk needs daily handling, and it spread from the riding peoples of the steppe.
- **Verdict:** leave to a time limit (confidence medium).
- **Why:** No row in the tree stands for what was missing: an elite able to keep full-time falconers, and the steppe riding culture the practice spread from. Its origin is uncertain but probably Ancient (firm records before 700 BC), so a window that opens one era early under the time limit matches history.
- **Notes:** If a prerequisite is wanted anyway, the nearest row is Specialization of Labor (SO05, CORE, Ancient), standing for the full-time falconer; it gives the same Ancient start. Horse Domestication (AH03) fits the steppe story, but its Grassland/steppe access condition would wrongly bar other civilisations. The Classical label is defensible: the practice spread in the Classical era and probably originated in the Ancient. No dependents. Sources: [IAF, A history of falconry](https://iaf.org/a-history-of-falconry/), [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/for-millennia-falcons-have-flown-through-the-art-of-many-cultures-180979693/).

### Advanced Pyrotechnics (F07) — CORE, Medieval, now available from Ancient

- **Represents:** Saltpetre pyrotechnics, the fire drug before the gun. It grants the Fireworks Ground (+1 Culture, +1 Happiness; requires Saltpeter) and is the prerequisite of Gunpowder (WA10) unless the world is GUNPOWDER_ABSENT.
- **History:** Precursors were incendiaries without saltpetre: Thucydides' fire of coals, sulphur and pitch at Delium (424 BC), and Greek fire (c. 672 AD; Greek Fire, F06). The row's own achievement needs the oxidiser. The earliest saltpetre and sulphur formula is Qingxuzi's (808), followed by a mid-9th-century Daoist warning against such mixtures, the recipes of the Wujing Zongyao (1044) and Song fireworks. In Europe come Roger Bacon (c. 1267) and Marcus Graecus (c. 1300), and Italian festival fireworks by the 15th century. Charcoal and sulphur had been available since the Ancient era; what was missing was saltpetre.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add Saltpeter Mining (MI08, CORE, Medieval), then available from Medieval (confidence high).
- **Why:** Pyrotechnics are saltpetre chemistry, and the row's own Fireworks Ground already requires Saltpeter, which only MI08 reveals. Without it, the row reveals itself in the Ancient era with a building it cannot use. Adding MI08 puts the row in its own era and matches the Tang and Song record.
- **Notes:** Gunpowder (WA10), the only dependent, already requires MI08, which then becomes redundant in WA10's cell, as O08 already is. MI08 exists in every world F07 does. - **History:** Precursors: variolation, meaning inoculation with smallpox matter, practised in China (firmly recorded by the 16th c.), India, the Ottoman lands and West Africa. Lady Mary Wortley Montagu brought it to London and Mather and Boylston to Boston, both in 1721. The dairy belief that cowpox protects against smallpox was acted on by Benjamin Jesty for his own family (1774). The row's own achievement: Edward Jenner's trial on James Phipps (1796) and his published Inquiry (1798), after which vaccination spread worldwide within a decade. Once anatomy and herd animals existed, what held it back was the idea of inoculation and, above all, the experimental method that turned a milkmaid's belief into a tested, published, repeatable procedure.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add Scientific Method (PH04, CORE, Early Modern), then available from Early Modern (confidence high).
- **Why:** The milkmaid observation was folk knowledge for decades before 1796. What turned it into vaccination was Jenner's tested and published trial, in the Royal Society's experimental tradition, which was also how variolation reached Europe. Scientific Method (PH04) is CORE and Early Modern (the row's own era), and it exists in every world and under every disease paradigm (vaccination needs no germ theory). It requires only Formal Logic (PH01), so there is no cycle.
- **Notes:** Scientific Method (PH04) is itself on the early list: it requires only Formal Logic, so it is available from the Classical era. As a CORE row its price premium keeps its discovery near its own era, but any prerequisite added to it will also apply to Vaccination. Contagion Experiments (MD15) is not suitable, since it is Pasteur's flask (1859–62), sixty years after Jenner. Variolation, the real precursor, has no row. No dependents; the Animal Husbandry dual path is unaffected.

### Anaesthesia (MD07) — STANDARD, Modern, now available from Medieval

- **Represents:** Surgical general anaesthesia. Every unit heals +5 HP whenever it heals, anywhere ('surgery in the field', v3.86). No dependents.
- **History:** Precursors: drugs taken by mouth to cause sleep (opium, mandrake, henbane; Hua Tuo's mafeisan, c. 200 AD; the medieval soporific sponge, 9th–13th c.). Ether, made by distilling alcohol with oil of vitriol (Valerius Cordus, 1540; Paracelsus saw it put chickens to sleep). Nitrous oxide (Priestley, 1772); Davy reported in 1800 that it killed pain and suggested using it in surgery. Hanaoka Seishū's herbal general anaesthetic in Japan (1804). The row's own achievement is inhaled surgical anaesthesia: Crawford Long's ether operations (1842), Horace Wells's nitrous oxide (1844–45), Morton's public ether demonstration in Boston (16 October 1846) and Simpson's chloroform (1847). Europe adopted it within months.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add Oxygen Theory (CH05, CORE, Early Modern), then available from Early Modern (confidence medium).
- **Why:** Distillation (CH04) and its acids already yield ether. What anaesthesia then waited for was pneumatic chemistry, which supplied controllable inhaled agents and the practice of breathing gases as medicine (Priestley's nitrous oxide, Beddoes's Pneumatic Institution, Davy's 1800 proposal); Oxygen Theory (CH05) is the tree's chemistry of gases. The remaining gap, 1800 to 1846, came from attitudes and demand: pain was thought useful and insensibility feared, surgeons were prized for speed, and the push finally came from commercial dentistry in the United States. No row can carry that, but with CH05 the row opens only one era early, which the proposed rule allows.
- **Notes:** No dependents. CH05 is CORE, exists in every world (phlogiston is a model, not a world: v3.71) and does not depend on MD07. Organic Chemistry (CH07, Modern) would put the row in its own era, but it is not what held anaesthesia back: ether (1540) and nitrous oxide (1772) came before it, and only chloroform (1831) came from it. The row's window (half the Modern era, about 30 turns) would open with CH05 in the Early Modern era, so it can still close before the Modern era begins.

### Existentialism (PH09) — UNCOMMON, Modern, now available from Classical

- **Represents:** +2 Culture from every Artist (the movement finds its voices in novels and plays) and existential-crisis events. No dependents.
- **History:** Precursors: Ecclesiastes; Augustine's Confessions (c. 400); Montaigne's Essays (1580); Pascal's Pensées (1670). The row's own achievement began with Kierkegaard (Either/Or 1843, The Concept of Anxiety 1844, Postscript 1846), then Dostoevsky (Notes from Underground 1864), Nietzsche (The Gay Science 1882), Heidegger (Being and Time 1927) and Jaspers (1932). The movement proper: Camus (1942), Sartre (Being and Nothingness 1943; 'Existentialism Is a Humanism', 1945) and de Beauvoir (1947–49); the name was coined in the mid-1940s. What held it back after Aristotle's logic: until the Enlightenment's secular critique (Encyclopédie 1751–72, Kant 1781) and Hegel's all-embracing system (1807–31), questions of meaning, death and freedom were answered within religion, and Kierkegaard wrote against Hegel. The movement's mass following came with modern mass society and the two World Wars, which are social conditions, not rows.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add The Enlightenment (PH14, STANDARD, Early Modern), then available from Early Modern (confidence medium).
- **Why:** Existentialism is an answer to the Enlightenment: it presupposes reason and science displacing religion as the frame of life, and its first author wrote against the rationalist system that followed. The Enlightenment is the one row that stands for this. It exists in every world and does not depend on Existentialism, and because Existentialism is UNCOMMON the new prerequisite need not be CORE. It leaves the row one era early; the twentieth-century flowering has no row and is left to the time limit.
- **Notes:** No dependents, so nothing else moves. Formal Logic [PH01] would become redundant, since The Enlightenment already requires it. The Enlightenment needs the Renaissance [CU07], which needs Letter of Credit [TR05]: three STANDARD rows in a chain, so Existentialism would be within reach in fewer games than its UNCOMMON tier assumes. If that matters, Scientific Method [PH04] gives the same era with no chain (CORE, Early Modern: the disenchanted, infinite cosmos whose silence frightened Pascal).

### Prosthetics (MD08) — UNCOMMON, Modern, now available from Classical

- **Represents:** Artificial limbs, used as a military benefit: every newly trained land unit gets the Medic promotion ('the wounded return to the line'). v3.84 gave the row this effect because it had none. It is a prerequisite of Cybernetic Enhancement (MD12).
- **History:** Prosthetics are ancient. The Cairo toe (wood and leather, 950–710 BC; replica tests show it helped its wearer walk), the Capua leg (bronze over wood, c. 300 BC), and Pliny's Marcus Sergius Silus, who lost his right hand in the Second Punic War (218–201 BC) and fought on with an iron one. Articulated limbs with springs and ratchets came in the Early Modern era: Götz von Berlichingen's iron hand (after 1504), and Ambroise Paré's mechanical hands and locking-knee legs (1564). What the Modern era added was an industry. Limbs were fitted by the tens of thousands after the American Civil War (1861–65: about 60,000 amputations, roughly three in four survivors; A. A. Marks's rubber foot, 1863; governments paid for the limbs) and after the First World War. Research programmes after 1945 and myoelectric hands then led to cybernetics.
- **Verdict:** era or content looks wrong (confidence high).
- **Why:** Nothing held prosthetics back once anatomy and gearing existed. They are a Classical achievement, which is exactly where the row's prerequisites already put it, and Silus going back to the line is the effect's own flavour. The Modern label fits only the industrial limb trade, which was driven by amputation becoming survivable at scale (anaesthesia 1846, antisepsis 1867) and by the demand of industrial war, not by a missing technology. Either make the row Classical and keep its present prerequisites, or keep it Modern as that industry and gate it on a Modern enabler of survivable amputation: Anaesthesia (MD07), itself under review, or the DISEASE_THEORY token.
- **Notes:** The dependent, Cybernetic Enhancement (MD12, RARE, Atomic), also needs Integrated Circuit (EN03) and Composite Materials (MS01), so its timing is unaffected either way. Antibiotics (MD06, Germ worlds only) later gives the same Medic promotion to every Military unit, so this row is its earlier, land-only form; whether that is too strong for the Classical era is a balance question. As a Classical UNCOMMON row, its window would be the Classical era's and would open in its own era. An Early Modern label (Paré's mechanical limbs) would still leave it two eras early on these prerequisites. The high confidence is in the history; which era to choose is Tamsin's call.

### Fractal Mathematics (MA11) — UNCOMMON, Atomic, now available from Early Modern

- **Represents:** +1 Science from Mine tiles (terrain read as mathematics). No dependents.
- **History:** Precursors: Weierstrass's nowhere-differentiable function (1872), the Cantor set (1883), Koch's snowflake (1904), Sierpiński (1915), Hausdorff dimension (1918), Julia and Fatou's iteration of rational maps (1917–20), Lévy (1938), and Richardson's coastline measurements (published 1961). The row's own achievement: Mandelbrot's 'How Long Is the Coast of Britain?' (1967), the word 'fractal' (1975), the Mandelbrot set plotted at IBM (1980; Brooks and Matelski's first plot was in 1978) and The Fractal Geometry of Nature (1982). By about 1920 the mathematics was largely in place; its listed prerequisites had been complete since non-Euclidean geometry (c. 1830). What held it back was the computer: Julia sets and the Mandelbrot set could not be seen until machines iterated them, and Mandelbrot's programme ran on IBM's.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add Programmable Computer (CP01, CORE, Atomic), then available from Atomic (confidence high).
- **Why:** Computer graphics turned the 'monsters' of 1872–1920 into a geometry of nature; without a computer the row's idea would not have occurred. The Programmable Computer exists in every world (through either switch), does not depend on Fractal Mathematics, and puts the row in its own era. Chaos Theory [MA14] already takes it for the same reason.
- **Notes:** No dependents, so nothing else moves. Topology [MA13] (Cantor's set theory, Hausdorff's dimension) would be a closer mathematical parent than Non-Euclidean Geometry [MA08], but it does not change the era.

### Information Theory (PH10) — CORE, Atomic, now available from Early Modern

- **Represents:** Great Scientists earned 10% faster (information made measurable). CORE because the Programmable Computer [CP01] requires it.
- **History:** Precursors: Morse code's short signs for common letters (1838–44); Boltzmann's statistical entropy (1877); Nyquist on telegraph speed (1924, 1928) and Hartley's logarithmic measure of information (1928), both in the Bell System; Johnson's discovery of thermal noise in valve amplifiers (1926; Nyquist's theory 1928); Szilard (1929); Shannon's relay-circuit thesis (1937) and his wartime cryptography memo (1945). The row's own achievement: Shannon's 'A Mathematical Theory of Communication' (1948) and his secrecy paper (1949). Leibniz's binary (1703) and formal logic had existed for centuries. What held the theory back was electrical communication: the telegraph (1837–44), the telephone (1876), and above all valve-amplified telephony and radio (the triode in 1906; valve repeaters on the transcontinental line in 1915). Their bandwidth and noise posed the questions the theory answers. It also needed a mature probability theory and the war's cryptography and fire control.
- **Verdict:** add prerequisite: add Vacuum Tube (EN01, CORE, Modern), then available from Modern (confidence high).
- **Why:** The theory is the mathematics of valve-age communication: the noise in its capacity law was found in valve amplifiers, and its bandwidth questions came from the repeatered telephone and radio. The Vacuum Tube is CORE, shared craft in every world, and does not depend on Information Theory. It leaves the row one era early.
- **Notes:** Its only dependent, the Programmable Computer [CP01], already cannot be had without the Vacuum Tube in either world (through the Transistor [EN02] or Resonant-State Computing [QM-E03]), so no other row gains an ancestor. The clockwork route into Computing (MC14, MC18) does not pass through Information Theory. Shannon's theory is probabilistic, but Probability Theory [MA06] and Statistics [MA09] are STANDARD and cannot be mandatory for a CORE row; Wireless Transmission [EL09] (telephone and radio) is the closest STANDARD row. Caveat: making it a prerequisite of the first programmable computer is a design choice, since Colossus (1943–44) and ENIAC (1945) predate Shannon's paper.
