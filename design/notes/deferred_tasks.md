## 2026-10-15: Decide what an Endeavour on an idea under study does with the study already done
- **Surfaced during:** v3.121, which lets Grand Endeavours target ideas already under study again (Tamsin: bomb shelters, the turn word comes that a rival has the bomb).
- **Location:** §1.5, *Under study or not*.
- **Issue:** as the simulator has it, the Endeavour starts from nothing at 2× the price while the idea stays under study. The Insight already spent studying the idea counts for nothing, and if study finishes first the Endeavour ends and its own Insight is lost. Measured (the expert, familiar world, `endeavour_oracle.py`): counting the study already done towards the Endeavour's price would make it beat funding the idea's focus for 15% of ideas under study instead of 12%, and in the funded focus for 22.5% instead of 13.5%.
- **Options:** keep it as it is; count the study already done towards the Endeavour (the idea then leaves ordinary study); and either way, decide what becomes of the Endeavour's Insight if study finishes first.
- **Tamsin's proposal (15 October):** count the study already done *before* the 2× premium, so the premium multiplies only the remaining work: 2 × (price − study done).
- **Measured (workshop report, *Endeavours that count the study already done*):** an Endeavour becomes the faster route for 18% of ideas under study instead of 12% (in the funded focus, 38% instead of 13.5%). **The catch:** the price is only the meter's average, and an idea still unfinished after long study probably has a large meter: at 90% of its price it still needs 47% of its price on average, and at its whole price 45%, where the rule charges 20% and nothing. So past three-quarters of its price an Endeavour is cheaper than study for 63% of ideas, and once an idea has had its whole price it is free: a careful player could cut off every unlucky meter, and the Endeavour would stop being insurance. In whole games the cost rule hardly matters to the simulated players: allowed Endeavours on ideas under study, the novice and the average player lose 4–6 turns under every rule and the expert nothing, since stalling the rest of the focus is the cost.
- **Suggested fix (Claude):** the same rule with a floor, "twice the work remaining, never less than the price": 2 × max(price − done, half the price). It tracks twice the expected remaining work at every stage, keeps most of the gain (sooner for 17% of ideas; 27% in the funded focus), is never free, and is cheaper than study for 7.5% of ideas (2.8% today).
- **Tamsin's second proposal (15 October), recommended by Claude:** the price ignores the hidden factor, so charge 2 × the price × the share of the idea's hidden meter still to fill. Measured: sooner than funding its focus for 16% of ideas under study (28% in the funded focus), never free (an idea with nothing left is discovered), cheaper than study only when the meter came out above twice the price (5.4% of ideas under study), which today's Endeavour already is for a fresh idea. Whole games: 296 / 305 / 379, like every other rule. The one consequence: the Endeavour's price shows how much of the hidden meter is left.
- **Tamsin's aim (15 October): an urgent button.** Endeavours should be viable as "I need this urgently", at the cost of overall progress (the Manhattan Project: every nuclear physicist pulled into one room). Her model: while an Endeavour runs, 70% of the turn's Insight goes into the breakthrough, 20% to the spread, 10% is lost; no premium. Measured (workshop report, *An urgent button*): for an idea sharing its focus, sooner than switching focus 77% of the time, in about two-thirds of the time; for a lone idea, funding its focus stays better (80% against 70%). Whole games: the racing expert uses it 13 times a game and wins 7 turns sooner (290 against 297); a cooldown of 10 turns between Endeavours brings every player to about 2 turns sooner than today (295 / 299 / 372, none unfinished), the 2 turns coming from no premium on ideas that have not occurred.
- **Tamsin's ideal (15 October), which 70/20/10 misses:** an Endeavour should reliably get the breakthrough sooner *and* make the game take longer to win. A cost paid while the Endeavour runs falls on research the race is not waiting for; it has to come afterwards and exceed the time saved. **Measured: a time debt.** An urgent Endeavour on an idea under study takes all the Insight into the breakthrough, no premium; when it is done, no focus is funded for 1.25× the turns it saved against funding the idea's focus. It beats funding the focus for 89% of ideas whose study would take two turns or more, typically in half the time, and every simulated player who uses it finishes later: 303 / 306 / 380 against 297 / 301 / 374, none unfinished. Claude recommends it in place of the cooldown below.
- **Then (Tamsin):** the time debt is neither intuitive nor fun, and no message may say how long study would take. Costs in Insight (abandoning the field's or the nation's other progress) do not slow a racer at all, since late-game Insight is plentiful; a recovery period was rejected as the debt by another name. **Tamsin's test (workshop report, *What a racer gives up*):** on all 15 victory conditions, the expert racing with 70/20/10 wins 6 turns sooner (291 against 297); at the same turn it lacks about 10 of the breakthroughs the non-racer holds, 5 of them economic (late power plants, laboratories, the Stock Exchange, the Dam), while holding about 6 the non-racer does not (its build rows, sooner). 70/10/20: 292, lacking 12–13, 6 economic. **Why so small:** an Endeavour runs a median 37 turns a game, but development (the era clock that brings the ahead-of-time premium down, and in the simulator sets income) still counts lost Insight, and the race is paced largely by development: a non-racer wasting 10% or 20% of its Insight from the Atomic era loses only 3 or 6 turns, but withholding it from development costs 9 or 14 (10%: 5 with income following all Insight produced). **Found the same day: an Endeavour's Insight not counting towards development** (`ENDALLNODEV=1`) makes the racer slower than the non-racer in most games, 306 against 296 (300 with income following all Insight produced, as in the real game), while each Endeavour still delivers its breakthrough as fast. Endeavour costs apply the ahead-of-time premium (Tamsin), fixed at commitment. Next: Tamsin decides.
- **Earlier recommendation, superseded:** 70/20/10 with a 10-turn cooldown. Still to decide: whether ideas that have not occurred keep a premium (without one, a glimpse can be bought at its price and luck counts for less), and whether the 70% fills the true meter (length uncertain) or a known price (the meter's share; the same results).
- **Also to correct with the next change to the document:** v3.121's changelog quotes 15% and 22.5% for counting the study done after the premium; with every Endeavour taking at least a turn, they are 14% and 22%.
- **Next:** Tamsin decides; §1.5 is written to match.

## 2026-10-15: Decide the terrain conditions on CORE rows
- **Surfaced during:** Tamsin's question whether the Windmill should need the Water Mill, and her point that the whole industrial chain waits on it.
- **Issue:** four CORE rows carry a terrain condition, which trade cannot meet (§1.2 lets trade meet resource conditions only), and two of them gate the FTL race:
  - **Irrigation (FA01), "River or Water source":** 170 rows cannot be had without it, 29 of them CORE (152 and 25 before v3.115). A civilisation with no river or water source can never win in any world: Orbital Mechanics, whose Apollo Program enables spaceship parts, needs Irrigation. "Water source" is not defined anywhere.
  - **Glassmaking (OP01), "Desert or Coast tile in territory":** 152 rows, 27 CORE (142 before v3.115): lenses, the Telescope, the Vacuum Tube, the Transistor and the Programmable Computer, Germ Theory, Radioactivity, Nuclear Fission, the quantum chain, Quantum Gravity Theory, Orbital Assembly. An inland temperate civilisation loses the FTL race in most worlds.
  - **Reed/Log Raft (SF01), "Coastal or River location":** 21 rows, all naval except the Windmill (through Sail Invention) and the Flying Boat.
  - **Horse Domestication (AH03), "Grassland/steppe access":** 5 rows, none CORE, among them the Horseman, the Chariot Archer and the Knight.

Glassmaking's one benefit is a Lighthouse bonus, which needs a coast anyway, and history agrees: inland Europe made glass from river or quarry sand and wood-ash potash (medieval forest glass; Bohemian crystal). The Raft can keep its condition if the Windmill takes Weaving in place of Sail Invention. Horse Domestication gates nothing CORE, so it can stay, though a civilisation with no grassland gets no Horsemen or Knights even with imported Horses. A battery check could hold the rule: no terrain condition on a row that a CORE row cannot be had without.

Nothing on the FTL path in any world, fossil-free ones included (Selective Breeding has a route through Botanical Classification). The battery passes.

With water the fix costs nothing (expert 289 against 290).   - **History:** good for the Workshop (Cornelis Corneliszoon's wind-powered sawmill of the 1590s, whose crankshaft turned the sails' rotation into the saws' strokes) and the Steam Engine (the Zaan's industrial windmills). Thinner for the Blast Furnace and Plate Armour, which took water power, and in dry country animal power, which has no row.

- **Issue:** in the bare roll model — without diffusion, trading, proximity revelation or Endeavours — a typical game misses 18–19 STANDARD ideas, which lock about 48 further rows (median 44, worst 108): roughly a tenth of the tree, in every world. - **Suggested fix:** larger than the FTL pass, so decided on its own. The most robust direction: (0) give many breakthroughs alternative prerequisites, so that no single missed idea can lock a branch — more routes to every technology. A simpler candidate: (0a) W16's funded-focus rule applied tree-wide — an idea's occurrence window stays open while its focus is funded, so only incidental research can miss ideas; it makes funded STANDARD ideas unmissable, which changes what the tier means. Narrower measures: (1) a structural rule that no STANDARD row may alone guard a large branch — such rows become CORE or gain alternative routes; (2) the parked general pity mechanic (2026-09-24 entry, "Add bad-luck protection to discovery rolls"), judged on what remains after (1); (3) investigate FA03's miss rate and the window compression behind it first. Diffusion does recover very old rows (e.g. O02 Weather Prediction) for civilisations with contacts, long before the late game; the lasting risk is in later gateways.
