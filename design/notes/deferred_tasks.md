## 2026-10-15: Decide the terrain conditions on CORE rows
- **Surfaced during:** Tamsin's question whether the Windmill should need the Water Mill, and her point that the whole industrial chain waits on it.
- **Issue:** four CORE rows carry a terrain condition, which trade cannot meet (§1.2 lets trade meet resource conditions only), and two of them gate the FTL race:
  - **Irrigation (FA01), "River or Water source":** 170 rows cannot be had without it, 29 of them CORE (152 and 25 before v3.115). Through Canal Building and the Water Mill it gates the Workshop, the Steam Engine, the Blast Furnace and the Factory's row (since v3.115), the Electric Generator, the Internal Combustion Engine, the rockets, Orbital Mechanics, Orbital Assembly, the Nuclear Fission Plant, Quantum Gravity Theory and Plenum Gravitation. A civilisation with no river or water source can never win in any world: Orbital Mechanics, whose Apollo Program enables spaceship parts, needs Irrigation. "Water source" is not defined anywhere.
  - **Glassmaking (OP01), "Desert or Coast tile in territory":** 152 rows, 27 CORE (142 before v3.115): lenses, the Telescope, the Vacuum Tube, the Transistor and the Programmable Computer, Germ Theory, Radioactivity, Nuclear Fission, the quantum chain, Quantum Gravity Theory, Orbital Assembly. In atomic worlds every FTL method needs it (Quantum Gravity Theory for four, Quantum Mechanics Formalism for Tunnelling); in elemental worlds only Warp and Wormhole avoid it. An inland temperate civilisation loses the FTL race in most worlds.
  - **Reed/Log Raft (SF01), "Coastal or River location":** 21 rows, all naval except the Windmill (through Sail Invention) and the Flying Boat.
  - **Horse Domestication (AH03), "Grassland/steppe access":** 5 rows, none CORE, among them the Horseman, the Chariot Archer and the Knight.
  - Iron Smelting's "iron deposits in territory" is a resource condition that trade or conquest meets; Specialization of Labor's population condition is open to everyone.
- **Why nothing caught it:** Guarantee Rule 1 promises a CORE path to Interstellar whatever the World Truths, and says nothing about terrain; the battery passes these rows, and the simulator ignores environmental conditions.
- **Precedent:** v3.93 removed exactly this kind of condition from the Charcoal Blast Furnace (three forest tiles): "The row's condition goes. The knowledge is open to every civilisation", and its building kept the condition.
- **Suggested fix (Claude's recommendation):** the same rule for every row a CORE chain runs through: the knowledge carries no terrain condition, and the condition lives on the benefit. Irrigation's farm bonus already applies only to Farms on Fresh Water, and Unciv's Water Mill must be on a River. Glassmaking's one benefit is a Lighthouse bonus, which needs a coast anyway, and history agrees: inland Europe made glass from river or quarry sand and wood-ash potash (medieval forest glass; Bohemian crystal). The Raft can keep its condition if the Windmill takes Weaving in place of Sail Invention. Horse Domestication gates nothing CORE, so it can stay, though a civilisation with no grassland gets no Horsemen or Knights even with imported Horses. A battery check could hold the rule: no terrain condition on a row that a CORE row cannot be had without.
- **Tested (15 October): Tamsin's alternative,** which keeps Irrigation's condition. The Windmill needs Weaving instead of Sail Invention, and the Blast Furnace, Crank and Connecting Rod, the Steam Engine and Plate Armour take the Water Mill or the Windmill (`WINDFIX` in `scratch_patch.py`).
  - **Structure:** Irrigation then gates 21 rows, all bound to water: the Water Management focus, the water UNIQUEs, Hydroponics and Vertical Farming, Miasma Engineering, and the optional Early Steam chain (Hero's Aeolipile needs Canal Building). Nothing on the FTL path in any world, fossil-free ones included (Selective Breeding has a route through Botanical Classification). The battery passes.
  - **Light run** (familiar world, a civilisation without water, `DRY=1`): without the fix the expert wins 0 of 300 games. With it, 299 of 300 (expert) and 296 of 300 (average), at the same medians as with water (290 and 380); every unfinished game is a STANDARD build-list idea, never the Windmill. With water the fix costs nothing (expert 289 against 290). A CORE Windmill (`WINDFIX=2`) measures the same, 299 and 297 of 300; the candidate model brings a missed idea back when a ready row needs it. Only a CORE Windmill keeps Guarantee Rule 1's all-CORE path for a dry civilisation.
  - **History:** good for the Workshop (Cornelis Corneliszoon's wind-powered sawmill of the 1590s, whose crankshaft turned the sails' rotation into the saws' strokes) and the Steam Engine (the Zaan's industrial windmills). Thinner for the Blast Furnace and Plate Armour, which took water power, and in dry country animal power, which has no row.
  - **Not fixed by it:** Glassmaking's "Desert or Coast tile". The Qanat System, the desert's own irrigation, still needs Irrigation's water source.

## 2026-10-15: Decide how far the One-Step Horizon reaches
- **Surfaced during:** Tamsin's question why the research-screen mockup needs a "Not yet reachable" status (a check of the document by three independent readers).
- **Location:** §1.4 (*The One-Step Horizon rule*, *CORE breakthroughs reveal themselves*), §1.5, §7.0 (`Reveals`), the v3.58 changelog entry.
- **Issue:** the document gives the rule two ways. §1.4 lets a breakthrough be Revealed when its prerequisite breakthroughs are "Discovered or Revealed", so a revealed row can wait on a prerequisite that is itself only revealed (and §1.5's Endeavour condition, "Revealed and all its prerequisites Discovered", only bites if it can). The next paragraph, and the v3.58 changelog, call "all prerequisites Discovered" "the furthest reach the One-Step Horizon permits", which rules that out.
- **Also:** even on the strict reading, the document's own reveals reach past the frontier: EL08 Baghdad Battery reveals EL02 "a millennium early" (EL02 needs CH04, a Medieval row); the decisive-experiment laboratories reveal rows whose prerequisites may be missing (TH10's lab → TH03, which needs ME12; CH19's → CH06, which needs CH05; MD15's → MD05, which needs OP03; CH25's → CH11, which needs CH09 or CH09-W); RC08 validated → RC09, which needs MS02; Endeavour spin-offs. Nothing says whether a reveal the rule blocks waits or is lost (`Reveals` is a one-shot Triggerable, so as written it is lost). The rule counts only breakthroughs: a revealed row whose environmental or resource prerequisite is unmet (TH19's tundra; a lapsed trade) cannot progress either. §1.4's own examples of theoretical revelation do not match the rows: Atomic Model → Nuclear Weapons is two steps (NP05 needs NP03); Oxygen Theory → Steam Engine is stale (MC08 needs PH04, not CH05); Electromagnetism → Telegraph is not a direct application (EL04 needs EL02 + SO04); none of NP02, CH05, EL03 or RC02 carries a Reveals list.
- **In the mockup:** the two "Not yet reachable" rows were added by hand (the simulator cannot make such rows). Rigid Airship fits the literal rule. Gravitational Waves Detection's source, "Revealed by General Relativity", does not hold: RC02 has no Reveals list, and when it was discovered (turn 186) the Laser was not yet revealed (turn 200), so the rule would have blocked the reveal.
- **History (from the claude.ai transcripts):** Claude proposed the horizon on 10 July 2026, inside the three-state model and its four triggers, answering Tamsin's wish for "a chance to unveil upcoming technologies as you get close to discovering them". She accepted the package with "Yes"; the horizon itself was never discussed. The same proposal wanted rows known long before they are reachable (gravitational waves, "a century of knowing it's there") and Endeavours "once a breakthrough is Revealed and its prerequisites are met". So §1.4's "Discovered or Revealed" is the original intent, and v3.58's "furthest reach" line is the slip (that day's message said only that the horizon *allows* CORE's reach).
- **Brainstorm in progress (15 October).** Claude's earlier recommendation of the strict reading is withdrawn: it would remove the long-visible targets Tamsin asked for. The question on the table: (A) rows known long before they are reachable, or (B) a reveal always means "you could start this now". Three models: 1, only what you could start; 2, a connected known map (a row can be revealed once each prerequisite is discovered or revealed, and a reveal that comes too early waits); 3, model 2 for what scholars infer, but what is seen or stolen ignores the horizon. Claude leans to A in model 2's form, with few sources (hand-written predictions, observation). Other ideas raised: Scientific Method as a switch (STANDARD rows reveal themselves once eligible); a dozen authored "famous predictions"; glimpses as "the goal, not the solution"; a Great Scientist revealing the next step in a focus; espionage stealing a concept; "promising developments" when an idea occurs unseen.
- **Already true under either option:** CORE self-revelation shows some rows long before they are affordable (the Scientific Method from Formal Logic, in the Classical era, at the era premium). Option A adds targets whose path is incomplete.
- **Set aside for this analysis (Tamsin):** EL08's early reveal, first-draft design; CH15's and OP09's reveals, made redundant by CORE self-revelation. All three go to the RARE and UNIQUE review.
- **Next:** Tamsin chooses; then §1.4 and §1.5 are written to match, §1.4's examples corrected, and the mockup's status kept or removed.

- **Issue:** Unciv's calendar is data — Standard runs 4000 BC to AD 2050 over 500 turns (40 years a turn to turn 75, then 25, 20, 10, 5, 2, 1 and 0.5) — and is almost entirely cosmetic: the top-bar year, the Maya's Long Count (a Great Person every 394 years), the Year countable, and later-era starts through `startPercent`. Since v3.108 the fork's game length matches base Civ V's at every skill level, so keeping the Standard schedule gives the same dates base does for each kind of player. An era-aligned calendar would put each era's start on its historical date, but for one skill level only.
- **Suggested fix:** choose whether to keep Unciv's schedule or write the fork's own; if the latter, pick the skill level it aligns to and derive years per turn from the redrawn era lengths (good player, about 30/31/32/37/52/60/49/29 turns): roughly 33 years a turn in the Primitive era from 4000 BC, 81 Ancient, 47 Classical, 12 Medieval, 7.5 Early Modern, 1.75 Modern, 1.1 Atomic and about 1 Space. The calendar would then speed up from the Primitive to the Ancient era; starting nearer 8000 BC (about 167 years a turn in the Primitive era) keeps it falling throughout. Settle the Space era's length (FTL re-audit) first, and rescale the schedule for every game speed.

## 2026-10-02: HIGH PRIORITY — stop missed STANDARD ideas from locking large branches of the tree
- **Surfaced during:** the FTL endgame redesign (direction document, W16), by two harness sweeps on 2026-10-02 (the sweep script was lost in a container reset; it can be rebuilt from the conversation, and its figures are recorded here)
- **Location:** §1.2 discovery mechanics (the occurrence window: a non-CORE idea has about half an era to occur once eligible, compressed when the civilisation is ahead of the row's era, and is lost for good if the window closes); the gateway rows below
- **Issue:** in the bare roll model — without diffusion, trading, proximity revelation or Endeavours — a typical game misses 18–19 STANDARD ideas, which lock about 48 further rows (median 44, worst 108): roughly a tenth of the tree, in every world. The damage concentrates in gateway rows that alone guard large branches: FA03 Selective Breeding (Ancient; guards 21–22 rows; missed in 35% of games — more than twice the designed ~15%, probably because its window is compressed when it becomes eligible late for its era), CH20 Transmutation Practice (Substance worlds' elemental chain; 21), CH22 Five-Phase Theory (Process worlds'; 24), MA06 Probability Theory (21), CO11 Reinforced Concrete (20), MA09 Statistics (20), MC11 Assembly Line (15–16), CP07 Artificial Intelligence (Narrow) (14), MC12 Robotics (14). Knowledge diffusion is no general safety net: it fires only once more than half of a civilisation's contacts already have the breakthrough, never for an isolated civilisation, and never for a race leader.
- **Suggested fix:** larger than the FTL pass, so decided on its own. The most robust direction: (0) give many breakthroughs alternative prerequisites, so that no single missed idea can lock a branch — more routes to every technology. A simpler candidate: (0a) W16's funded-focus rule applied tree-wide — an idea's occurrence window stays open while its focus is funded, so only incidental research can miss ideas; it makes funded STANDARD ideas unmissable, which changes what the tier means. Narrower measures: (1) a structural rule that no STANDARD row may alone guard a large branch — such rows become CORE or gain alternative routes; (2) the parked general pity mechanic (2026-09-24 entry, "Add bad-luck protection to discovery rolls"), judged on what remains after (1); (3) investigate FA03's miss rate and the window compression behind it first. FTL is protected separately by W16's funded-focus rule for the Space era (adopted 2026-10-02, replacing the provisional CORE-like rolling of hypothesis paths). Diffusion does recover very old rows (e.g. O02 Weather Prediction) for civilisations with contacts, long before the late game; the lasting risk is in later gateways.
- **Measured cost to FTL (2026-10-13):** with a lone civilisation on the candidate discovery model, a competent detective, and games allowed to run on past the end of the tree, about 40% of games (12 of a 30-game atomic sample) can never build their viable FTL method. W16 cannot rescue them (it covers the Space era only), nor can an Endeavour (it needs a Revealed row), so for an isolated civilisation this breaks the guarantee that victory is never dice-locked. Found while testing Endeavours as insurance; the same games had been silently dropped from earlier engineering and victory times (`power_tools/w7f.py` now records why a game never finished its build).
- **Re-measured under the agreed settings (2026-10-14; `design/simulator/power/missed_ideas.py`, `results/missed_ideas_<world>.txt`):** before the Space era, a connected civilisation (five rivals, the 10% glimpse) loses about 4 STANDARD ideas a game, which alone lock about 4 rows (p90 9); with an Endeavour for every glimpsed one, 0.4. Proposed in the workshop report's *Where it stands*: keep the bounded window and treat the gateways as tree content (option 1).
- **A candidate fix in the model itself (2026-10-14):** Tamsin's fading attention for STANDARD ideas (no closing moment; attention to a stale open question fades instead). Measured: missed STANDARD ideas before the Space era fall from 17.6 to 2.9 a game isolated (locking 2.7 rows, worst 9) and from 4.1 to 2.3 connected; every FTL benchmark game finishes, and the victory medians do not move at today's prices. See the workshop report, *Fading attention*. **Rejected by Tamsin, 14 October 2026**: the closing window stays, so this entry's other options remain the way forward.
- **Option (0a) measured (2026-10-14):** applied to the whole tree, the funded-window rule barely changes anything. Missed STANDARD ideas go from 4.0 to 3.9 a game with contacts and from 17.2 to 17.3 isolated; locked rows are unchanged within the noise. Three things limit it. One focus in 34 is funded at a time. The player cannot aim it at ideas that have not occurred. And a held idea closes as soon as the player moves on. - **Rediscovery on demand (2026-10-14), awaiting Tamsin's decision:** a lost STANDARD idea comes back with 10% a turn for each breakthrough, or FTL prototype, ready but for it. Missed leaves stay missed (the intended variance), but chains stop being locked: isolated, rows locked behind missed ideas fall from 23 to 1.4 a game (worst 7), and every FTL game finishes for all three players, with contacts or isolated, even with glimpses of ideas that have not occurred switched off.
