Smoothing the discovery rate at parity (15 Oct 2026). Tamsin: "can we tweak the era boundaries to smooth out the discovery rate
through the game, or would that bring development-derived eras wildly out of parity with technology-derived eras?"; then "yes,
run it - count rivals' knowledge too. It's fine for later technologies to be on average more turn-expensive than earlier ones".
The aim: no era faster than one discovery a turn, counting rivals' knowledge, for all three players; parity with base Civ V's
science victory kept (within 5 turns of 305 / 305 / 380); development's eras still aligned with the eras players enter by
section 2's counts.

Screens: familiar Warp, Process Tachyonic and Substance Plenum, 60 games a player each (180 a player), PYTHONHASHSEED=0, no game
unfinished anywhere. Made by smooth_screen.sh <label> [Era=x ...] [span.Era=y] [VAR=value ...], with rivals rebuilt under each
candidate's prices (make_pool.sh) unless the label says otherwise. Settings from smooth.py: each era's price per discovery x its
multiplier against v3.108's; up to the Early Modern era an era's span of development moves with its price, as section 2's rule
has it (span = price x expected discoveries), and idea windows follow the new era lengths; the Modern, Atomic and Space
boundaries stay at base Civ V's timing (v3.122). Victory as victory.py has it (prototype and Drive costed).
Pace: era_pace.py, turns per discovery while development is in each era, up to the race's finish; in brackets, the player's own
discoveries only. Alignment: the median turn a civilisation enters each era by section 2's count, minus the turn its
development does (positive: the count comes later).
The control, d0, is the parity setting (v3.122's era timing with the Modern, Atomic and Space prices at x1, rivals pb): on these
three conditions 302 / 306 / 380 (304 / 308 / 381 on all 15).

=================== Findings
1. A game holds about as many discoveries as turns. By the race's finish a player has made 280-308 discoveries on every screen,
   whatever the prices or diffusion: 0.97-1.07 turns per discovery over the whole game for the expert and novice, 1.21-1.32 for
   the average player. Players discover nearly everything that occurs to them; prices set how long that takes. With the
   game's length fixed by parity, era settings can only move the fast stretches around, not slow the game's discoveries as a
   whole.
2. The late eras can't be slowed at parity. They take 85% of the Insight spent on discoveries, so dearer late research
   lengthens the science victory and no early discount can buy it back: round 1 brought the expert's Modern and Atomic eras to
   about one a turn (its Space era stayed at 0.8 turns per discovery) at a cost of 6-20 turns against the control. v3.122's
   prices brought the late eras to about one a turn at a cost of 22-32 turns against base Civ V.
3. Slowing diffusion doesn't help. The player researches what it no longer receives, so the late pace barely moves while
   victory lengthens (against the control: doubled delays +4 / +7 / +4, tripled +7 / +13 / +12, four contacts of five
   +3 / +7 / +7, all five +4 / +8 / +7, none at all +9 / +24 / +26). Diffusion is how the novice and the average player keep up.
4. The early game can be smoothed at parity, at a cost in era timing. The Ancient era is the fast stretch there: the novice
   makes about 40 discoveries in its 32 turns (32 Ancient rows, 7 left-over Primitive ones). e2-e7 bring every era up to the
   Early Modern to at least one turn per discovery for all three players (e1, the Ancient era x1.5 alone, leaves the novice at
   0.9), with victory unchanged (300-303 / 303-308 / 376-380). The mildest that does it, e7 (Ancient x1.5 with its span, the
   Primitive span x1.3), keeps era entry aligned with development as today; but civilisations get through their Ancient rows
   later, so they enter the Ancient and Classical eras by count 4-7 turns later (Medieval 3-4, Early Modern 1-2). Development's Ancient era would end on turn 69
   (today 62; base Civ V's research passes the same stretch on turn 52 on the same income). The simulator's income doesn't
   depend on the breakthroughs held, so it can't see what later Ancient-era buildings would cost in play.
5. Under one discovery a turn in the Modern to Space eras, at parity, needs fewer discoveries there: about a fifth fewer (the
   expert and novice make about 140 between development entering the Modern era and the race's finish, in about 110 turns).
   Since the count is set by what occurs, not by price, that means fewer late rows reaching a player: merging rows into bigger
   breakthroughs priced at the sum (the victory's research then costs the same, so long as each merged pair is either both on
   the way to the victory or both off it), dropping rows, or rarer late ideas (STANDARD occurring in fewer than 85% of games,
   which changes what the tier means).
