The Space era as the endgame: windows that never close, with a chance a turn set directly (16 Oct 2026, v3.126).
Tamsin: "Let's try treating the space era differently, as the endgame. Test what happens if space era windows never close, and
rather than calculating discovery chances from window length, adjust them directly as a lever."

== The rule (harness_ftl_e.P.space_open / space_chance; w7f.py SPACEOPEN=1, SPACEOCC=p or tier:p,...)
- A Space-era idea's window never closes, so no Space-era idea is ever lost and rediscovery is never needed there.
- A Space-era STANDARD idea occurs with chance p each turn once its breakthrough is discoverable (p is the lever), instead of the
  chance derived from its window (today 28 turns, 6.6% a turn, 85% within the window). UNCOMMON, RARE and UNIQUE Space-era ideas
  keep their chances a turn (0.91%, 0.19%, 0.12%) and also never close; their windows already span the Space era, so that changes
  almost nothing within a game's length.
- Settings: VIS = today's rules; S066, S10, S15, S20, S30, S50, S100 = SPACEOCC 0.066 (today's chance a turn), 0.10 ... 1.0.
- Every setting: players who decide on visible information only (VISIBLE=1, Tamsin's rule of 16 Oct: the expert knows the fixed
  foundations, the novice only the screens), rivals made under the same switches (make_pool.sh <world> <label> VISIBLE=1 ...),
  all 15 victory conditions, three players, 60 games a cell, 2,700 games a setting, TOVICTORY=1 SAVE_T=1, PYTHONHASHSEED=0.
  The baseline (VIS) reproduces visible_players_20261016.txt exactly.

== 4. Reading
- Never-closing windows alone (S066, today's 6.6% a turn) narrow the spread from 17.8 to 14.1 but lengthen the science victory by
  2-3 turns (312 / 314 / 389): today a stale idea comes back by rediscovery on demand, 10% a turn for each thing waiting on it,
  which is sooner than it would occur at 6.6%.
- Raising the chance narrows the spread quickly to about 10 at 15% a turn and 8-9 at 20-30%, then little more (8.3 at 50%, 7.1 at
  100%). It also shortens the science victory, but by less: every setting from 10% to 50% is within the 5-turn limit for all three
  players (10%: 305 / 308 / 381; 15%: 302 / 305 / 379; 20-30%: 301 / 303 / 377; 50%: 300 / 302 / 375, the average player at the
  edge); at 100% the expert is 6 turns short. So the rule also cures the visible-information players' overrun (+5 / +7 / +6
  today) without any price change.
- What the chance cannot fix: from 15% up, the method furthest from its field is Plenum or Tunnelling, slow, in every player and
  world. At 20-30%, Wormhole, Hyperspace and Tachyonic are within about 2 turns of their field, Warp 3-4 quick, Tunnelling 6-8 slow
  and Plenum 9-10 slow. Their routes run through rows that are no FTL requirement themselves but prerequisites of one (Aerospace
  Industry and Reusable Launch Vehicle for Plenum's Current Sail, Planetary Data Networks for Tunnelling's Quantum Computing), which the expert finds 17-30 turns after their prerequisites are met, and
  through study along dearer rows (Current Sail x2.5, Quantum Computing x2). That is a matter of guidance or of those rows, not of
  occurrence.
- The cost is the Space era's other content, because the race is shorter: non-FTL Space-era breakthroughs held at the launch fall
  from 22-24 a game today (a game 5-7 turns too long) to about 19-20 at 10%, 17-18 at 15%, 16-17 at 20% and 15-16 at 30%. The
  funded-focus rule at parity (visible_players_20261016.txt, VFP14) kept 13.5-15 with a spread of 4.7.
